Your domestic packaging supplier probably buys from China

By the Complete Sourcing team · October 5, 2026

Many U.S. packaging distributors resell clamshells and boxes made overseas, with a markup. Here's how to tell, what it costs you, and when going factory-direct makes sense.

Here is something most packaging buyers never check: a large share of the clamshells, blister packs and printed boxes sold by U.S. packaging distributors are made overseas — often in the same factories you could buy from directly. The distributor's value is convenience and small minimums. The cost is a markup you pay on every unit, every order, forever.

How the domestic model works

A typical packaging distributor buys containers of stock and custom packaging from overseas manufacturers, warehouses it in the U.S., and resells it with a markup that covers their warehouse, sales team and margin. That's a real service — for a brand buying a few hundred or a few thousand pieces, it's often the best option.

But for a growing brand buying 20,000, 50,000 or 100,000+ units a year, you're paying for warehousing and handling you don't need.

How to tell if your packaging is imported

  1. Ask. "Where is this manufactured?" is a fair question. Vague answers are an answer.
  2. Check the cartons. Master cartons often carry a country-of-origin mark.
  3. Look at import records. Public U.S. customs data shows which companies import packaging by the container — distributors frequently appear as importers from Chinese thermoforming and printing factories.

What going direct actually saves

When brands we work with compare a domestic quote to a factory-direct, landed quote (freight, U.S. duties, inspection included), the gap at production volume is commonly 30–50%. For example, custom folding PET clamshells we source run about $0.07–$0.08 per piece at the factory, and printed magnetic gift boxes about $0.49–$0.74. Even after freight and tariffs, those numbers usually beat domestic resale pricing by a wide margin at volume.

The honest caveat: savings only show up when you compare landed cost. A factory price that looks 70% cheaper can shrink to 20% after freight and duties — or vanish entirely on a tiny order.

Why brands don't do it themselves

Price isn't the obstacle; risk is. Buying direct means finding a trustworthy factory, managing samples across time zones, checking quality before you've paid in full, and navigating ocean freight, customs and changing tariffs. One bad container can wipe out a year of savings.

That's the gap a packaging sourcing agent fills: you get factory-direct pricing with someone accountable for quality, freight and duties — and one landed price per piece.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my packaging is made overseas?

Ask your supplier directly where it's manufactured, check the master cartons for country-of-origin marks, and look at public import records for your supplier's company name. Many distributors are importers.

How much can I save buying packaging factory-direct?

At production volumes, brands commonly land 30–50% below a domestic distributor's delivered price — but only after freight, duties and inspection are counted. Small orders often don't save money.

Is buying direct from China risky?

It can be without help: quality problems, miscommunication and surprise customs bills are the common failures. Third-party inspection, clear specs and DDP shipping remove most of that risk.

When should I stay with my domestic supplier?

If you order a few thousand pieces or less, need very fast turnaround, or use a stock item that's already cheap, a domestic supplier is often the better choice.
