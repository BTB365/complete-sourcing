What drives rigid box pricing

  • Size — more board, more paper, more freight space.
  • Closure — magnetic hinged lids cost more than two-piece lid-and-base boxes.
  • Finish — matte or gloss lamination is standard; soft-touch, foil, spot UV and embossing add cost. Scratch-proof (anti-scuff) lamination is worth it for dark boxes.
  • Inserts — EVA foam, sponge, paperboard or molded pulp.
  • Quantity per design — splitting 44,000 boxes across four designs still priced like volume because the structure was identical.

Freight: why boxes cost more than their price tag

Rigid boxes ship assembled and take up a lot of space. On one shipment of about 65,000 boxes (523 cartons, roughly 64 cubic meters — a full 40-foot container), duties-paid ocean freight was $8,500, about 13 cents per box. On another, a 40-foot container of ~76,000 two-pack boxes ran about $16,500. Freight rates swing a lot by season and year, so always compare a DDP landed price.

Tariffs matter too: paper boxes from China carried an additional 25% tariff when we shipped in 2024. That's another reason to quote duties-paid.

Avoiding the expensive mistakes

  • Approve a physical sample, not just a digital proof — color and magnet strength never look the same on screen.
  • Specify anti-scuff lamination on dark colors.
  • Inspect before shipping. A pre-shipment inspection catches color drift, weak magnets and crushed corners while you can still fix them.
  • Fill the container. Ordering a few more months of boxes to fill a 40-foot container often costs less per box than a partial shipment.
