Clamshells and blister packs both put your product on display, protect it and let it hang on a peg. But they are built differently, cost differently and need different equipment to pack. Here's how to pick.

The quick difference

ClamshellBlister card
What it isOne-piece plastic shell folding closed around the productPlastic blister heat-sealed to a printed paperboard card
Packing equipmentNone (snap/lock closed)Heat-sealing machine
BrandingEmbossed logo and/or paper insertFull printed card front and back
Tamper resistanceHigh (especially sealed)Medium
Cost per unit at very high volumeMediumLowest
RecyclabilityEasy (mono-material PET)Harder (plastic + paper)

Choose a clamshell when…

  • You don't have (or don't want) heat-sealing equipment at your 3PL.
  • The product is heavy, sharp or high-value and needs a tougher shell.
  • You want the product fully visible from both sides.
  • You want a cleaner, mono-material package for recycling.

A trifold clamshell is a popular middle ground: a third panel holds a printed insert card, so you get branding and a barcode without sealing.

Choose a blister card when…

  • You sell at very high volume and every fraction of a cent matters.
  • You want a large printed card for retail messaging.
  • Your packer already has sealing equipment.

What drives the cost of each

For both formats, the big levers are size, material thickness, the one-time mold, and — often overlooked — freight. Formed plastic ships bulky, so a design that nests well can save more than a cheaper material. We break down real numbers in how much custom clamshell packaging costs.

Our take

For most small and mid-size product brands, a custom folding or trifold PET clamshell wins on simplicity: no sealing, easy packing at the 3PL, tough protection and an embossed logo for free. Blister cards earn their place at very high volumes or when a printed card is essential for retail.
