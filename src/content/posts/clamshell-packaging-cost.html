If you are pricing custom clamshell packaging, the number a factory quotes you is only the start. Here is what clamshells actually cost — based on real production orders we have managed — and how to compare quotes on a fair, landed basis.

Factory price: what the plastic costs

These are factory prices from actual orders of custom folding PET clamshells (0.5 mm, mid-size retail product, two sizes sharing one program):

OrderQuantityFactory price / piece
First production run~80,000$0.070
Smaller follow-up run~14,000$0.083
Reorder, same mold~80,000$0.070

Notice two things. First, the price barely moved between orders a year apart — once a mold exists, reorders are predictable. Second, the small run cost about 19% more per piece. Volume matters, but not as dramatically as most people expect; freight is where volume really pays off.

What changes the factory price

  • Size and depth — more material and slower forming.
  • Thickness — 0.4, 0.5 and 0.6 mm are common. Heavier products need thicker walls; we confirm with samples.
  • Material — PET/RPET (our default), PVC or PETG.
  • Features — hang tabs, embossed logos, locking buttons, insert slots.

One-time costs: molds and samples

A custom clamshell needs a thermoforming mold. Expect anywhere from a few hundred to a couple of thousand dollars depending on size and cavities. Sample rounds (formed samples sent to you by express) typically cost $150–$300 each, and most projects take one to three rounds to get fit and wall strength right.

Tip: embossing your logo into the mold costs nothing per piece afterward — it's a cheap way to look custom without printing.

Freight: the hidden half of the price

Clamshells are light and bulky. A container fills by volume long before it gets heavy, so you are paying to ship space. On one order, the factory price of the clamshells was about $5,650 — and the duties-paid ocean freight for that shipment was $16,500. That is an extreme example (it shipped alone during a period of surging ocean rates), but it shows why you should never compare factory prices alone.

Ways to cut freight per piece:

  • Design for nesting so clamshells stack inside each other in the carton.
  • Consolidate clamshells with boxes or other goods to fill a container.
  • Order fewer, larger runs — a full container is far cheaper per piece than LCL.

Tariffs and duties

Plastic packaging from China carries U.S. duties plus additional tariffs, and the rates have changed several times in recent years. In 2024 our freight partner was quoting an effective tax rate of around 28% on plastic clamshells. Because rates move, we quote DDP (delivered duty paid) so the duty is locked into your price.

Inspection

A third-party pre-shipment inspection runs roughly $330–$750 per visit. On a clamshell run of 80,000 pieces that is under a penny per piece — and it catches cracked hinges, cloudy material and short counts before they reach your warehouse.

Putting it together: landed cost per piece

Cost lineTypical share
Factory priceLargest single line at high volume
Ocean freight + U.S. duties/tariffsOften equal to or more than the factory price for bulky clamshells
Inspection, card fees, misc.A few percent
Mold & samplesOne-time; spread across the life of the mold

The right comparison is landed cost per piece at your warehouse versus what your current supplier charges delivered. For many brands buying 20,000+ clamshells a year, factory-direct still lands well below a domestic distributor — but you only know once freight and duty are in the number.
